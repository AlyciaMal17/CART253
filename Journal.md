### **_Process Journal_** – 3 Events Prototypes P5 & GitHub (Week 6 - Oct 5th):

<p>This week I made three prototypes: Lightbulb, Stop Light, and The Barking Doorbell. They all use user input to change what happens in the sketch, but each one works a little differently. What surprised me the most was how one simple action, like pressing a key or clicking a button, could change an entire scene. In Lightbulb, holding the spacebar turns on the light, while letting go makes everything dark again. In Stop Light, clicking switches between red and green, which controls whether the car moves or stops. In The Barking Doorbell, clicking the button opens the door, turns on the light, and plays a dog bark before everything goes back to normal.</p>
<p>I thought it was cool how I could make the sketches feel more interactive by using different types of input. Adding sound and a timer to The Barking Doorbell was especially interesting because it made the scene feel more realistic and gave it a little surprise. I also liked how the car in Stop Light keeps moving and comes back on the other side of the canvas, which makes the scene feel like it never really ends.</p>
<p>The hardest part was understanding how to make everything happen at the right time and making sure each action worked properly. For example, The Barking Doorbell has to open when you click the button, stay open for two seconds, and then close automatically. I had to think about how different parts of the code work together instead of just focusing on one shape at a time.</p>
<p>Overall, I liked seeing how a few shapes, variables, and user interactions could turn into something more interesting. I wanted Lightbulb to feel like the light depends on you, Stop Light to make you feel in control of the car, and The Barking Doorbell to be funny and a little unexpected. If I were to develop one further, I would choose The Barking Doorbell because I think it would be fun to add different sounds or have different things appear behind the door each time you ring it.</p>

### **_Process Journal_** – 3 Function Prototypes P5 & GitHub (Week 7 - Oct 19th):

<p>This week I made three prototypes: Balloon Stand, Buzzing Flies, and Fish Tank. They all use functions to create and control objects, but each one has different interactions. In Balloon Stand, there are five coloured balloons that grow a little with each click, turn orange as a warning, and pop after the third click. In Buzzing Flies, four silly flies start off smiling, but when the mouse gets close to them, they turn red, look angry, and shake more. In Fish Tank, three colourful fish float peacefully in a bowl until you press the spacebar, which shakes the bowl and makes the fish turn green with X eyes and frowns.</p>
<p>I liked how each prototype had its own personality. For Balloon Stand, I wanted it to feel like a carnival game where you have to keep clicking until the balloons finally pop. For Buzzing Flies, I thought it was funny how the flies react when you get too close to them, while in Fish Tank, I wanted to show how something peaceful can change when you disturb it. I also liked experimenting with colours, movement, and facial expressions to make each sketch more interesting.</p>
<p>The hardest part was figuring out how to organize the functions and make everything work properly. For example, the balloons needed to change size and colour before popping, the flies had to react depending on the mouse's distance, and the fish needed to react to the bowl shaking. I learned how functions can help me create and control multiple objects without having to write the same code over and over.</p>
<p>Overall, I enjoyed seeing how I could use the same idea of functions in three different ways. If I were to develop one further, I would choose Fish Tank because I think it would be fun to add more fish, different colours, or more reactions when the bowl gets shaken.</p>

### **_Process Journal_** – 3 Arrays Prototypes P5 & GitHub (Week 8 - Oct 26th):

<p>This week I made three prototypes: Night Sky, Candy Catcher, and Annoyed Ghost. They all use arrays and loops to manage multiple objects or pieces of information, but each one has a different idea and interaction. In Night Sky, 30 glowing stars slowly move and twinkle across a gradient sky. When the mouse gets close, they scatter away and eventually settle back into place. In Candy Catcher, colourful candies fall from the top of the screen, and you have to click on as many as possible before the 45-second timer runs out. In Annoyed Ghost, a ghost goes trick-or-treating but nobody opens the door, and every click moves through his dialogue as he becomes more and more annoyed.</p>

<p>I enjoyed giving each prototype its own personality and atmosphere. I especially liked experimenting with the visuals, colours and little interactions to make each sketch feel different. For Night Sky, I wanted a calm and dreamy feeling, while Candy Catcher was meant to start off easy and become more chaotic. I also thought Annoyed Ghost was a fun idea because I could use dialogue to tell a little story and make the character's personality change over time.</p>

<p>The most interesting thing I learned was how arrays and loops can make it easier to manage multiple things without having to create each one separately. For example, in Night Sky, one array can hold all 30 stars, and the loop makes them all move and react individually. I also learned how arrays can store text, like the different dialogue lines in Annoyed Ghost. The more difficult part was understanding how to connect everything together, especially controlling the different interactions and making sure things happened at the right time. Candy Catcher was also interesting because I had to figure out how the speed of the candies could increase as the timer got closer to zero.</p>

<p>Overall, I think it's cool how something as simple as an array and a loop can create a whole experience or even a small game. I hope people who try my prototypes notice the little details and enjoy interacting with them rather than just looking at them. If I were to develop one further, I would choose Night Sky because I think it would be interesting to add more types of stars, constellations or even a moon, while keeping the same calm atmosphere.</p>
